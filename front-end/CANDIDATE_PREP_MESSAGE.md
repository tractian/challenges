Subject: Prep for your TRACTIAN System Design Interview

Hi [Candidate Name],

Ahead of your upcoming System Design interview with TRACTIAN, here's what to expect and how to prepare.

**Overview**

This module will focus on your ability to architect a system. Rather than writing out code line by line, you'll be asked to create the high level design for a larger domain. This primarily takes place as a whiteboarding exercise, but expect lots of clarifying questions. What are the tradeoffs of using one implementation over another? How does this system scale under load? What's a way to improve performance for our users? What's the best way to model and store this data? You'll want to consider how various systems work together, such as the interaction between the client, server, and storage layers.

We will provide a link to a virtual whiteboard, please make good use of the design space! It's helpful to think about it as a collaborative exercise with a peer. We are working to build a design proposal which could be handed off to a teammate, so we should include whatever level of detail is needed to ensure they are successful.

**What we're looking for:**
- **Comprehensiveness** — ensure your approach covers all aspects of the functional requirements and tackles any potential edge cases.
- **Feasibility** — ensure your solution is practical and could realistically be implemented.
- **Scalability** — ensure your solution has the capacity to scale as we increase users or broaden the problem requirements.
- **Resilience** — real world systems experience failures as a matter of course, how well does your system tolerate outages?

**Interview Tips:**
- **Clarifying Questions** — before jumping in to designing your solution, ensure that you understand all aspects of the given requirements.
- **Trade Offs** — share your rationale behind using one approach over another.

**Logistics**
- Format: live whiteboarding session, no coding required
- Duration: 1 hour
- Link to the whiteboard will be sent by the interviewer

Looking forward to the conversation — come ready to ask questions and think out loud.
